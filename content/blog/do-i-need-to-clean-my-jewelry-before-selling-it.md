No. You don't need to clean your jewelry before you bring it in. Bring it just as it is.

## Why shine doesn't change the metal value

Tarnish, dirt, and everyday wear don't change how much gold, silver, or platinum is in a piece. We test purity and weight using professional equipment, then price against real-time precious metals market rates. A dull chain and a polished one with the same purity and weight are worth the same as metal.

## Coins are different: please don't clean them

If you have old coins, leave them exactly as they are, tarnish and all. Collectors generally prefer coins in their original condition, and scrubbing or dipping an old coin can change its surface and lower what it's worth, sometimes significantly. Some coins are worth more as collectibles than as metal, so a cleaning that seems harmless could cost you money. If you're unsure whether a coin is special, bring it in untouched and we'll tell you which is which.

## If you'd still like to tidy up

For jewelry, a gentle wipe with a soft, dry cloth is plenty. Skip abrasive cleaners, polishing compounds, and anything harsh. They can scratch a piece and won't change what we pay for the metal.

## Broken, tangled, or mismatched? Bring it anyway

You don't need to untangle a chain, find the missing earring, or have anything repaired first. Broken chains, mismatched earrings, and damaged pieces are all evaluated on their metal content and value.

## What to bring

Your items, just as they are, and a valid government-issued photo ID, as required by North Carolina law for precious metals transactions. No appointment is necessary.
